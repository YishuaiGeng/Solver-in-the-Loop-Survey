Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) increasingly interact with symbolic solvers, but the empirical benefits of this integration remain difficult to compare across tasks and evaluation protocols. This survey systematically reviews 1,459 studies identified through database searches and first reported between January 2020 and October 2026. We organize solver integration into five non-exclusive functional roles, distinguished by how the system uses the solver’s output: symbolic solving, verification, diagnosis and repair, search control, and learning supervision. We distinguish these roles from the interaction mechanisms that realize them, characterized by topology, invocation control, intervention granularity, and information exchange. Synthesizing selected within-study comparisons across logical reasoning, optimization, planning, theorem proving, and program verification, we find task-specific gains from symbolic computation, diagnostic feedback, and solver-derived supervision, whose direction and size depend on the base model, the search budget, and the task; additional feedback or training does not improve every evaluated configuration. We distinguish successful proof construction for fixed formal targets from the acceptance of generated representations, whose fidelity to the original task requires separate assessment, and we separate improvements under retained inference-time checking from transfer without the checker. The synthesis identifies research priorities in semantic validation, adaptive intervention, and the evaluation of computational cost and generalization.
